In Alzheimer's disease the protein tau clumps into filaments inside nerve cells. In 2017 Sjors Scheres, Michel Goedert and colleagues at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology used cryo-EM to determine the structures of tau filaments extracted from the brain of a person who had died with Alzheimer's disease, revealing their ordered core in near-atomic detail. The filaments turned out to have a defined fold. Later work from the same groups found that different tau diseases have different folds, opening the way to classifying neurodegenerative diseases by the structures of their filaments.
