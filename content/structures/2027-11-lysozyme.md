Lysozyme is an enzyme in tears and egg white that breaks down bacterial cell walls; Alexander Fleming discovered it in 1922. In 1965 David Phillips and colleagues at the Royal Institution in London solved its structure, the first of any enzyme. For the first time, researchers could see the cleft where an enzyme grips its substrate, here a chain of sugars, and propose atom by atom how it cuts it. Lysozyme became one of the most studied proteins in structural biology, and hen egg-white lysozyme crystals are still a favourite test sample at synchrotrons and in crystallography teaching.
