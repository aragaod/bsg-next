Haemoglobin carries oxygen in our blood. Max Perutz began studying its crystals in Cambridge in 1937, and in 1960 his team published a three-dimensional structure showing four subunits, each folded much like John Kendrew's myoglobin. Higher-resolution structures of the forms with and without oxygen later showed how the molecule changes shape to pick up and release oxygen cooperatively. Perutz and Kendrew shared the 1962 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and Perutz went on to chair the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology, which opened that year.
