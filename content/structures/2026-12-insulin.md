Insulin controls blood sugar, and its structure was a quest of more than three decades for Dorothy Hodgkin in Oxford. She took her first X-ray photographs of insulin crystals in the 1930s and, with a team including Tom Blundell and Guy and Eleanor Dodson, solved the structure in 1969. By then she had already received the 1964 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the structures of penicillin and vitamin B12. The insulin structure showed how the hormone is stored as zinc-containing hexamers, and informed later work on modified insulins used by people with diabetes.
