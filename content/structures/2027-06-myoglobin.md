In 1958 John Kendrew and colleagues in Cambridge published the first three-dimensional model of a protein: myoglobin, which stores oxygen in muscle. At that low resolution it looked like a tangle of sausages. Within two years they had reached atomic detail, revealing the alpha-helices that make up most of the molecule and the haem group that holds the oxygen. Kendrew shared the 1962 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Max Perutz, whose team solved haemoglobin, starting the field that today spans crystallography, cryo-EM, SAXS and computational prediction.
