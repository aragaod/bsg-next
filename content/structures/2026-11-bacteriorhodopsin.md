Bacteriorhodopsin is a light-driven proton pump from salt-loving archaea, packed so densely in their membranes that it forms natural two-dimensional crystals. In 1975 Richard Henderson and Nigel Unwin at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology used electron microscopy of these crystals to see, for the first time, seven helices crossing a membrane. In 1990 Henderson and colleagues built an atomic model from electron cryo-microscopy data. The methods developed along the way laid foundations for today's cryo-EM, and Henderson shared the 2017 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing cryo-electron microscopy.
