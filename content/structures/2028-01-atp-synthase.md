Almost every cell makes its energy currency, ATP, with a molecular machine called ATP synthase. In 1994 John Walker and colleagues at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge solved the structure of its F1 part from cow heart mitochondria. The three catalytic subunits were caught in different states, one empty, one holding ADP and one holding an ATP analogue, just as Paul Boyer's 'binding change' mechanism predicted. This supported the idea that a central stalk rotates to drive ATP synthesis, like a motor. Walker and Boyer shared the 1997 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
