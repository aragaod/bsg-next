Targeted protein degraders are drugs that remove a disease-causing protein rather than just blocking it, by bringing it to the cell's own disposal machinery. Many work through cereblon, the protein bound by thalidomide-related drugs such as lenalidomide and pomalidomide. Cereblon is hard to work with on its own, so Alessio Ciulli's group at the University of Dundee designed a stable, simplified version, CRBN-midi. With it they solved crystal structures with degrader drugs such as mezigdomide, and used small-angle X-ray scattering at Diamond to check how it behaves in solution.
