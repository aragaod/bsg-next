On 25 April 1953 Nature published three papers on the structure of DNA. James Watson and Francis Crick in Cambridge proposed the double helix, drawing on X-ray fibre diffraction data from King's College London, including an image taken in Rosalind Franklin's lab by Raymond Gosling, now known as Photo 51. Papers from Maurice Wilkins, Alexander Stokes and Herbert Wilson, and from Franklin and Gosling, appeared alongside. The structure immediately suggested how genetic information is copied. Watson, Crick and Wilkins received the 1962 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine; Franklin had died in 1958.
