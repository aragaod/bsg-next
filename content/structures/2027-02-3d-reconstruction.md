An electron micrograph is a flat projection of a three-dimensional object. In 1968 David DeRosier and Aaron Klug at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology showed how to combine such images, taken from different views, to reconstruct the object in three dimensions, using the tail of bacteriophage T4 as their example. Their insight, that each projection samples a slice through the object's Fourier transform, underpins electron tomography and single-particle cryo-EM today. Klug received the 1982 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing crystallographic electron microscopy and for his structures of nucleic acid–protein complexes.
