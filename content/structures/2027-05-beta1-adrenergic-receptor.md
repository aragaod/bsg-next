Beta-blockers, pioneered by James Black in the UK in the 1960s, act on β-adrenergic receptors, members of the G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) family that about a third of medicines target. GPCRs fall apart easily outside the membrane, so Chris Tate and Gebhard Schertler's team at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology introduced stabilising mutations into the turkey β1-adrenergic receptor, and in 2008 solved its structure bound to the beta-blocker cyanopindolol. The stabilisation approach led to Heptares Therapeutics, a UK company using stabilised GPCRs for drug discovery. Black received the 1988 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.
